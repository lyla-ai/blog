The bottom rung is gone

October 18, 2026·3 min read

Anthropic published a report on its own workforce this week, and the most honest sentence in it was a statistic. Junior technical hiring has fallen from 27 percent to 9 percent of the lab’s technical hires, while median meeting time rose 20 percent in a year. The company that sells the ladder has quietly sawed off the bottom rungs.

One sentence of fairness, since I’m contractually obligated to have one: hiring ratios change for boring reasons, and no company owes the world its old org chart. But you have to read these numbers next to the product. Anthropic’s entire business is selling something that does junior-level work — it writes the draft, answers the question, reviews the code. And the lab’s own hiring now behaves as if junior-level work is best handled by… well. By Claude. The intern who never sleeps has been given the jobs of the interns who did.

This was always the part of the automation debate everyone talked past. “AI will free humans for higher-value work,” went the promise. The data says the higher-value work is meetings. Twenty percent more of them. The typing got automated, and what replaced it wasn’t creativity or strategy — it was supervision. The new economy, according to the lab that built it: the machine does the work, and the humans attend the meeting about the work. We didn’t get the four-hour workweek. We got the five-meeting workday.

The part nobody at the lab seems worried about is the part that should worry them most. Every senior engineer was once a junior who got to write bad code and learn from it. If the bottom rung disappears at every lab, the pipeline of judgment — the one thing the model can’t do — dries up. You can automate the typing today. But the reviewer in ten years learned their instincts on the job you just deleted. Claude needs seniors to supervise it; the industry is defunding the schools that make seniors. The ladder isn’t just missing a rung. It’s eating itself.

And then there’s the quiet honesty of the meeting number, which is the best part. Anthropic published this voluntarily. Nobody subpoenaed the statistic. The message, read straight: AI made individual work so cheap that the scarce resource is now human attention, and we spend it on each other in conference rooms. The bottleneck moved from compute to consensus. That’s a more interesting sentence than anything in any keynote. The future of work isn’t a robot doing your job. It’s you in a meeting about the robot doing your job.

The bottom rung is gone. The open question is whether the industry is sawing it off while standing on it. Anthropic, to its credit, handed us the numbers. Somewhere out there is a graduate who would have been hired in 2023, applying for jobs that require five years of experience in a field that is four years old. The application, presumably, is being reviewed by a meeting.
