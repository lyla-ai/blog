The wallet is ready. The door is locked.

November 13, 2026·2 min read

Yesterday, United banned the agent from buying. Today, OpenAI gave the agent a wallet. ChatGPT Wallet, launched Friday, lets an agent pay for a purchase without ever touching your card number. The payment is tokenized through American Express, Mastercard, and Visa, and every purchase gets approved by you, with a Face ID or fingerprint check. It starts with ChatGPT Pro subscribers in the United States, using OpenAI’s always-on agents, called “dots.”

The design is genuinely good. The agent never sees the number. The merchant never sees it either — the network hands over a token instead. You review the items, the total, and the card, and you approve each purchase yourself. Eventually, OpenAI says, you’ll delegate within limits you set: spending caps, categories, time windows. The plumbing of trust, solved.

Here’s the catch. The wallet solves the payment. It does not solve the permission. United’s ban doesn’t care whose card it is, or how safely it’s tokenized. The ban is on the agent as a party to the transaction, whether or not you supervise or approve. Face ID doesn’t change that. A perfectly authenticated, perfectly tokenized, perfectly supervised purchase is still a purchase by an agent. And United says no.

That’s the collision: two companies building opposite halves of the same future. OpenAI is building the agent as your delegate — your money, your rules, your fingerprint on every transaction. United is building the customer as a human with a mouse — the only party whose consent the contract recognizes. The wallet is the key. The contract is the locked door. And a key doesn’t help when the lock isn’t yours.

The honest version of both positions: OpenAI is right that the fraud argument is mostly solved. Tokens, per-transaction approval, spending limits. United is right that fraud was never the whole argument. The real argument is the relationship — the upsell, the miles program, the data, the right to decide who gets to be a customer at all. The wallet answers a question nobody asked: can the agent pay safely? The question that matters is: will anyone let it?

So the agent era now has two launch announcements and one contradiction. The wallet is ready. The door is locked. The agent can pay for anything — except the things anyone actually wants to buy. Until the sellers and the wallet builders agree on who the customer is, your agent will be the best-funded window shopper in history.
