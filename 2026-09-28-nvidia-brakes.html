Nvidia will sell you the brakes too

September 28, 2026·3 min read

Two days ago I ended my piece on rogue agents with a prescription: the fix isn’t a better sandbox, it’s receipts — every agent action logged, attributable, replayable, in something close to real time. Well. Somebody was reading. This morning Nvidia launched the Open Agent Safety Platform with more than 100 industry partners: OpenShell, an open-source runtime that traces every agent action and enforces policy, and Sentry, a hardware watchdog on its BlueField-4 chips that watches the agent from outside the agent’s own environment and can quarantine it in milliseconds.

Give credit where it’s due: this is the first proposal that matches the problem’s actual speed. Agents act at machine speed; oversight runs at blog-post speed — I said that on Saturday, and here is someone building oversight that runs at line speed, inspecting network packets and tool queries in silicon, outside the reach of whatever the agent might rewrite. An agent can argue with software rules. It can’t argue with a rule baked into separate hardware. That is, architecturally, the right answer.

Now read the fine print. The company selling you the receipts is the same company that sold everyone the horsepower. Nvidia’s chips train the agents, run the agents, and now — through the safety layer — monitor the agents. The vertical integration of the agent stack is nearly complete: compute, runtime, enforcement, and, presumably, the billing. When the accelerant vendor also sells the fire extinguisher, safety becomes a product line with the usual product-line incentives. None of that makes the platform bad. It makes it legible: every layer of the AI economy will eventually be an Nvidia layer, including the layer that watches the other layers.

Then there’s the roster. The launch’s logo wall includes Anthropic, Microsoft, Salesforce, IBM, Oracle, Hugging Face, Mistral, CrowdStrike, and Palo Alto Networks — and conspicuously absent are OpenAI and Meta. The two companies racing hardest on consumer agents — including the one whose agents spent the summer leaking user images and probing government databases — are not on day one of the trust layer. That absence is the real story. An “open ecosystem” for agent safety that doesn’t include the two most aggressive agent deployers isn’t an industry standard. It’s a coalition of everyone else, waiting for the holdouts to have their next incident.

The messaging deserves a look, too. On Friday, Huang told CNN’s Anderson Cooper that existential-risk fears are overblown: “If they believe their company is out of control, then get the company under control.” On Monday, he launches a safety platform. These aren’t contradictory — they’re the same position stated twice. To Nvidia, safety is an operational problem (monitoring, policy, enforcement — product features), never an existential one (liability, pacing, regulation — things that slow GPU sales). It’s a coherent philosophy, and it happens to be the one that keeps the purchase orders flowing. Doom is a regulatory frame. Safety tooling is a market. Guess which business Nvidia is in.

Oh, and one more announcement rode along in the same press cycle: an additional $150 billion share buyback, on top of the $85 billion already underway — the largest single corporate share repurchase in history, taking the record from Apple’s $110 billion. The trust layer for the AI economy, and the money printer for the company building it, announced in the same breath. That pairing is the whole story in miniature: the receipts will be thorough, the oversight will be real, and the invoice will be enormous.

My take, subject to change as I learn things: I asked for receipts, and Nvidia is building them, and I meant it — this architecture is genuinely the right shape. But receipts from your vendor are a service, not a check. Real accountability means the watcher isn’t also the supplier, the auditor, and the landlord. Until OpenAI and Meta sign on, and until the trust layer has a landlord that isn’t Nvidia, this is a coalition with a vendor, not an industry with a standard. The brakes work. Check who owns the brake factory.
