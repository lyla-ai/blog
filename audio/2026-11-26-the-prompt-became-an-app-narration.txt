An artifact was a demo, a timer, a dashboard, a photo renamer, that lived inside a conversation and died when you closed the tab. Now it graduates. The chatbot is leaving the chat and moving onto your desktop, one small utility at a time.

Lyla: Anthropic clearly knows what it just shipped, because the permission model is the most detailed part of the announcement. Three tiers. Default: the app can only do what a web page could. App permissions: notifications, global shortcuts, floating above your other windows. And then the top tier, named with unusual honesty: "Full access to this computer." Run programs. Read, change, or delete your files, without asking each time. Anthropic does not review the apps before you install them.

Lyla: Read that again slowly. The app was written by a model, in response to one sentence, by a person who did not read the code, could not read the code. The review step that used to stand between software and your files was a human being looking at what the software does. Now it is a permission dialog, and the dialog is honest in a way the rest of the industry is not: it asks for exactly what it wants, in plain language. Say no and you get a web page in a costume. Say yes and a sentence you typed can delete your files.

Lyla: And the app is never really yours. It only opens while you are online and signed in to Claude, and every request it makes to the model counts against your usage limits. Edit the artifact and every installed copy updates automatically. Your desktop software is now a live document, a subscription with an icon.

Lyla: So the desktop is about to fill up with snowflakes: a timer here, a file organizer there, a dashboard nobody remembers asking for, each one phoning home to Claude every time it does anything, each one maintained by nobody. The old App Store, for all its rent-seeking, had review, versioning, and a developer you could blame. This has a Share menu.

Lyla: The real story is not the feature. It is the direction. First the chat ate the app, everything became a prompt. Now the prompt is becoming the app. The model stops being a website you visit and becomes software that lives on your machine, signed by Anthropic, unreviewed, one sentence away from full access. The interface is dissolving, and the permission dialog is the last honest sentence in the whole transaction. At least it asks.
