The AI tax is due

October 13, 2026·2 min read

For years the pitch went like this: AI would make everything cheaper. Cheaper code, cheaper support, cheaper content — abundance, raining down on all of us. Then Intel quietly raised its prices for the third time in a year, and it turns out the abundance has a cover charge.

The numbers, courtesy of the supply-chain rumor mill. Intel’s high-end Panther Lake laptop chips: up 15–20% since September 1. Its older Meteor Lake and Arrow Lake chips: up 5–10%. Qualcomm’s Snapdragon X and X2 Elite: about 10%, effective October 1. AMD is reportedly considering its own 10% hike. And TSMC is expected to raise advanced-node prices again in 2027. Analysts estimate Intel’s chip prices are up close to 30% this year alone.

The reason is the punchline. The wafers are getting more expensive because the same industry building your laptop is diverting silicon capacity to AI servers — high-margin accelerators eating TSMC’s 3nm capacity like it’s the last slice of pizza. Your next laptop costs more because a datacenter is drinking your milkshake. Progress, invoiced monthly.

Here’s the thing nobody at the keynotes says out loud: this is the AI boom’s first mass-market product, and it isn’t intelligence. It isn’t cheaper software, smarter assistants, or any of the glowing futures from the slide decks. It’s inflation. The AI economy is running so hot it’s raising the price of the things that run everything else. The revolution will not be televised; it’ll be passed through to the bill of materials.

There’s a deeper joke underneath the business model. The pitch for AI is deflation: do more with less, software eats everything, fire half the floor. But the companies selling deflation are buying up the world’s most expensive chips at any price — and that scarcity lands on your credit card. AI isn’t making the computer cheaper. It’s making the computer more expensive so the AI can be cheap. Pick your poison; you’re paying either way.

So here’s the take: the AI era didn’t begin with a breakthrough. It began with a price hike. The future costs more than the present — not because it’s better, but because somebody has to fund the training runs. Welcome to the AI tax. There are no exemptions, and there is definitely no deduction.
