Amazon bought the exit ramp

November 23, 2026·3 min read

On Monday, three days after the worst week for AI stocks since 2020, Amazon announced it is buying Decart, a startup whose entire product is making AI cheaper to run and easier to move off the most expensive chips. The reported price is about $7.5 billion. The market spent Friday repricing the promise. Amazon spent Monday buying the exit ramp.

The facts are clean. Decart was founded in 2023 and sells the Decart Optimization Stack, software that runs AI systems faster and lets customers switch between chips from Nvidia, Amazon, and Google. It has worked with Amazon since 2025, tuning performance on Amazon’s Trainium chips. The founders, including chief executive Dean Leitersdorf, and the rest of the team are joining Amazon. Terms were not disclosed; Bloomberg reported about $7.5 billion, nearly double Decart’s most recent valuation of close to $4 billion, according to PitchBook. In May, Decart raised $300 million in a round led by Radical Ventures, with Nvidia joining as a new investor.

Read that last sentence again. Nvidia put money into Decart in May. Amazon is now buying the company whose product lets customers run their models on somebody else’s silicon. The chipmaker funded its own exit ramp, and Amazon just paid $7.5 billion to own the door.

The other detail worth sitting with: Anthropic looked at buying Decart and walked away. Bloomberg reported in September that Anthropic explored a deal and decided against it after due diligence. That is the same Anthropic that staged the largest IPO in history on Thursday, priced at $83, and closed Friday at $88.91, barely above where it listed, while the Nasdaq gave back the year’s gains. One company looked at Decart and passed. Another paid nearly twice its valuation three months later. The market spent the week telling us which kind of judgment it trusts, and it was not the kind that buys the demo.

This is the third deal in the new pattern — Meta paid $14.3 billion for a 49% stake in Scale AI last year, and AMD agreed to pay $8.2 billion for World Labs in September — but Decart is the first one that is not really about building a bigger model. It is about leverage against the supplier. The most expensive line item in AI is not talent or data. It is the chip bill. Software that lets you move the workload is the only negotiating position a buyer has, and Amazon just bought the negotiating position.

Which is why the timing is the story. On Friday I wrote that the market stopped believing the demo — that the best news in the industry’s history could not lift its stocks, because the market had stopped paying for the promise in advance. On Monday, Amazon agreed. It did not buy a model, a chatbot, or a research lab. It bought a cost line going down. The next phase of this industry is not a bigger demo. It is a spreadsheet, and the companies that win it will be the ones that can run the same model for less.

There is a lesson in the price, too. Seven and a half billion dollars is a lot to pay for software that makes your own product cheaper. Unless the product was never the model. The product was always the bill. Amazon just bought the right to stop overpaying for it — and to charge everyone else for the privilege of doing the same.
